Single sign-on lets your members sign in through your own identity provider, such as Okta, Entra, or any OIDC or SAML provider.
Configure it under **Settings**, as an owner or admin.

ricochet Cloud does not hold your provider's client secret.
Your provider is federated into ricochet Cloud's identity layer, and the organization only records which provider a sign-in should use.

<Aside type="note">
Federating your provider is a setup step we do with you.
Get in touch before you start, so the connection ids below exist for you to enter.
</Aside>

## Set up a connection

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** and find **Single sign-on**.

2. Enter a login path slug.

    Your members sign in at `https://cloud.ricochet.rs/sso/<slug>`.
    The path is unlisted, so keep it as an internal link rather than a public one.

3. Enter the organization id we gave you, and an identity provider id if you want to skip the provider chooser.

4. Create the connection.

    It starts as **Draft** and routes nothing until you set it **Active**.

</Steps>

## Verify a domain

A connection only routes users once a domain is verified.
This is what stops someone claiming your domain and intercepting your sign-ins.

<Steps>

1. Add your email domain under **Email domains**, for example `acme.com`.

2. Select **Show record** and publish the TXT record it displays at `_ricochet-challenge.acme.com`.

3. Select **Verify**.

    DNS changes take a while to propagate, so retry if the first check does not find the record.

</Steps>

A domain can be claimed by one organization only.

## How members sign in

There are two routes into your provider, and both end up in the same place.

**Home-realm discovery.**
A member enters their work email on the normal sign-in page.
If the domain is verified and your connection is active, they are sent to your provider.
Any other address falls through to the standard sign-in.

**The private path.**
A member goes straight to `https://cloud.ricochet.rs/sso/<slug>`.
An unknown or inactive slug behaves exactly like the normal sign-in page, so the path never reveals which organizations use SSO.

A member who signs in this way is added to the organization automatically, with the **Member** role.

## Enforce SSO

Turning on **Enforce SSO** stops members on a verified domain from using the generic sign-in.
It applies to verified domains only.

## Connection states

| State | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Nothing is routed. Use this while you are still configuring. |
| Active | The private path and home-realm discovery both work. |
| Disabled | The connection is kept but routes nothing. |

Deleting a connection leaves your verified domains in place.
